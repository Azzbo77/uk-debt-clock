Real-world context — Inflation, wages & opportunity cost
Debt interest opportunity cost
~£110bn / year
Interest is the third-largest public spending item. It exceeds the entire defence budget and is roughly half the NHS England budget. Every pound spent on interest cannot fund services, tax cuts or investment.
Real regular pay growth
~0.3% (recent)
ONS data for early–mid 2026 shows real regular pay (CPIH-adjusted) growing at only around 0.3%. Nominal wage growth has slowed while the debt interest bill remains large, limiting fiscal room for tax relief or higher public-sector pay.
House prices (nominal)
Low single-digit growth
Average UK house prices have risen only modestly in 2025–26 (around 1–2.5% annually depending on the index). After the high inflation of 2021–23, real house prices remain below earlier peaks in many areas, especially London and the South East.
What £4,090 per second means
£245,400 / minute
At the current extrapolated rate the debt rises by roughly £14.7 million every working hour. Over a typical 8-hour working day that is nearly £118 million added — money that future taxpayers will service through interest.
Interest vs taxpayer
~£3,235 each
Dividing the annual interest bill by ~34 million taxpayers gives roughly £3,200+ per taxpayer per year that goes to debt servicing before any public service is paid for.
Long-term pressure
OBR warning
The OBR’s July 2026 Fiscal risks report shows that without further action, demographic pressures (health, pensions) plus rising net interest could put debt on an unsustainable upward path from the 2030s/2040s.

Extrapolated at £4,090/sec
The debt counter ticks live using published ONS PSND data (series BKQK) fetched on page load and refreshed every hour. The constant £4,090/sec rate is an average derived from the latest full-year deficit projection (not a claim about daily cash borrowing, which fluctuates). Annual totals are updated 2–3 times per year in line with major OBR publications. Last successful ONS figure is cached locally so the page still works if the API is temporarily unavailable. All figures are indicative and for illustrative purposes only.
Important: This clock is an illustrative tool based on published ONS and OBR data. The national debt is a liability of the public sector — it is not a personal debt owed by individual citizens. "Debt per person" and "debt per taxpayer" figures are mathematical divisions used to give scale, not legal obligations. Governments do not typically aim to fully repay national debt; they manage it as a share of GDP over time. Data is subject to revision.

PSND vs PSNFL: This page tracks Public Sector Net Debt excluding public sector banks (PSND ex) — the traditional headline measure. The government currently targets a broader balance-sheet measure, Public Sector Net Financial Liabilities (PSNFL), which is lower because it includes additional financial assets. Both are published by the ONS.

For authoritative figures, consult the ONS Public Sector Finances and Office for Budget Responsibility.
